Why Polynomial Trendlines Fail for Aircraft Fuel Efficiency

A UK DfT report used 5th-order polynomials (likely Excel defaults) to model fuel burn vs. range for widebody aircraft. Toggle the fits below to see why that's the wrong functional form.

Why does this matter? Polynomials are flexible enough to fit almost anything within the data range, but they have no physical basis. They create spurious undulations that obscure real patterns—like the fact that aircraft types converge in fuel efficiency near 7,000 km. Worse, polynomials always diverge to ±∞ outside the data range. A power function y = a · xb + c is physically motivated—fuel burn per km falls as range increases, approaching an asymptote—and extrapolates sensibly.
